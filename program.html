Year-one curriculum

A year of building, not just talking about ideas.

The planned September-to-May curriculum contains 30 interactive, 45-minute meetings designed for a school lunch, advisory, or club period.

Five units. One progression.

Students gain the technical foundation first, then use it to solve a real problem with a documented prototype.

1. Become a Maker

Lessons 1-5: lab safety, tools, structures, forces, mechanisms, and the Fall Build Sprint.

2. Circuits & Electronics

Lessons 6-11: voltage, breadboards, inputs, sensors, outputs, and the Circuit Challenge.

3. Physical Computing

Lessons 12-18: microcontrollers, code, responsive devices, debugging, and the Winter Prototype Challenge.

4. Invent Something Real

Lessons 19-25: problem research, planning, prototype sprints, testing, iteration, and pitch preparation.

5. Teach, Showcase & Lead

Lessons 26-30: Teaching Fellow preparation, supervised Outreach Labs, reflection, and chapter handoff.

Lesson Library

Individual lesson pages, videos, slide decks, build guides, quizzes, and chapter downloads are in production.

What students learn

Technical skills

Mechanical design, circuits, breadboards, sensors, coding, microcontrollers, debugging, prototyping, testing, and technical documentation.

Inventor skills

User research, problem framing, teamwork, project planning, evidence-based iteration, clear pitching, and leadership.

What students make

From a bridge or mechanism to a problem-focused invention.

Across the year, teams create a mechanical build, an input-to-output electronics device, a responsive coded prototype, and a final invention project recorded in a shared Team Invention Portfolio.

Chapter resources

Full lesson packages, the Team Invention Portfolio, advisor and safety handbooks, competition resources, and maker-kit guidance are being prepared for founding chapters.
