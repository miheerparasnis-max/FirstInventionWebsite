Teach the next generation

Learn to build. Then help someone else build.

Teaching Fellows are high-school chapter members trained to make hands-on STEM feel welcoming, safe, and exciting for younger students.

What fellows learn

How to explain a build simply, ask useful questions, encourage productive struggle, share materials, manage a small group, and know when to bring in the responsible adult.

What fellows do not do

They do not run unsupervised sessions, handle permission or behavior issues alone, use unapproved tools or power, or take over a younger student's build.

Three Outreach Labs

1. Build a Circuit

Younger makers create a simple low-voltage LED circuit and experience their first debugging win.

2. Build a Moving Invention

Teams use safe common materials to create, test, and improve a moving mechanical prototype.

3. Test & Demo Day

Teams test what they made, make one improvement, and show how their invention works.

Safety comes first

Prepared fellows. Approved adult supervision.

Outreach Labs happen only after the elementary or middle-school host, adult supervisor, age group, space, materials, safety plan, registration, and permission process are confirmed. High-school students facilitate; the host and advisor retain adult responsibility.

Teaching Fellow resources

Readiness checklists, facilitation guides, micro-teaching practice, Outreach Lab plans, and host planning tools are prepared as founding-stage resources and will be posted after launch review.
